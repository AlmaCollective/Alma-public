## The Research Question

ALMA begins with a question about evidence, not about emotion:

**When has physiological evidence earned the right to surface as a human-facing offer — without transferring interpretative authority from the individual to the system?**

Most wearable technologies collapse detection and interpretation into a single step: a signal changes, the system infers a state, and acts on that inference — through an alert, a notification, or an automated classification. ALMA treats this collapse as the central problem, not a design detail.

More specifically: can a system that only offers — never asserts, never classifies, never notifies by default — support a person in recognizing their own physiological transitions earlier and with more confidence, without displacing their authority over what those transitions mean?

## The Problem

Physiological changes often begin before they become conscious experiences, symptoms, behaviors, or performance changes. Most existing systems focus on:

- continuous monitoring
- retrospective interpretation
- alerts and notifications
- automated classification

By the time a change becomes obvious, the underlying physiological transition may already be well underway.

The challenge is not collecting more data. The challenge is deciding, moment to moment, whether the system has earned enough confidence to say anything at all — and remaining silent when it hasn't.

## The ALMA Architecture

ALMA investigates this through a staged trust chain, where each stage must earn the right to continue:

**Signal → Observation → Pattern → Personal Threshold → Offer → Human Recognition → Bookmark (optional) → Meaning**

If confidence breaks anywhere in that chain, ALMA stays silent. Silence is the default state, not a failure mode.

## The ALMA Hypothesis

This architecture exists to test a further hypothesis: that physiological awareness may itself be trainable. Repeated interaction with a system that offers only when it has earned the right to — rather than one that classifies or alerts continuously — may gradually improve a person's ability to recognize their own physiological transitions earlier and with greater confidence.

Success is not dependence on technology. Success is capability transfer.

The long-term goal is not lifelong reliance on ALMA. The long-term goal is increasing independent physiological awareness — with the decision architecture as the mechanism, and trainability as the hypothesis it makes possible to test.

## Current Research Focus

ALMA currently investigates:
- physiological transition detection
- temporal organization of physiological dynamics
- voluntary self-bookmarking behavior
- recognition latency
- learning trajectories
- confidence-based haptic feedback
- individualized physiological baselines
- false-positive reduction
- capability transfer

Rather than asking only what a physiological signal means, ALMA asks two linked questions: when has that signal earned the right to speak, and can repeated, well-timed offers make humans better at recognizing meaningful physiological changes themselves.
